In a small church, one pastor carries every hospital visit, every grief call, and every homebound member. Often they drive straight from work, with no time to prepare. The visit happens, but it happens hurried. Emmaus Care exists to give that pastor ten quiet minutes of preparation.
Meet Margaret, a sample member, in hospital after hip surgery. One click drafts her whole visit plan: the heart of the visit, how to open the conversation, and what to listen for. Notice that the plan picked up the surgery, and her fear that God feels far away, straight from the notes.
It offers three Scripture options, from public domain translations, with a simple rule: read one slowly, only if the moment is right. It drafts a prayer with her name in it, ready to edit into your own words. And it names the mistakes to avoid, like promising a healing outcome.
The part churches most often miss is follow up. Emmaus Care schedules it before the visit: a message tomorrow, a family check in three days, help on discharge day, and a home visit in two weeks. One click prints it as a visit card, to carry in a Bible or a jacket pocket.
Two design promises. First, privacy: everything runs on this device. Notes never leave it, and the app asks for a first name only. Second, the human stays in charge: every plan is a draft to review, it never diagnoses, and in a crisis it points to real help first. Six kinds of visits, one calm screen, and follow up that does not fade. That is Emmaus Care: walk in prepared, listen first.
